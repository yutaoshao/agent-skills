# Reviewer Response and Revision

Use this module to turn reviewer or editor feedback into a revision and response letter.

## Map Every Comment to Evidence and Action

Create a working record before drafting the response. Preserve the reviewer's wording or a faithful summary, then distinguish a completed change from a proposed or infeasible one.

| ID | Reviewer concern | Evidence or premise | Decision | Completed manuscript change | Response evidence | Location |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

`Decision` may be address, clarify, partially address, or respectfully decline. A respectful decline explains the setting, evidence, or scope that makes the requested action unsuitable; it does not dismiss the concern. When a concern identifies a real gap, revise the manuscript first when possible and cite the new table, figure, analysis, or section precisely.

## Write the Response Letter

Group the response by reviewer and retain stable IDs for each comment. Quote or summarize the concern, state the direct answer, give supporting evidence, and identify the exact revision location. Use calibrated language: report completed analyses in the past tense, label work that remains planned as planned, and never describe an intended experiment as a completed result.

For each added or changed citation, verify the record and name the revised location. For revised numbers, figures, or tables, trace them to the corresponding result source. Keep the manuscript, response letter, change record, and any diff output synchronized.

## Close the Revision

Check that every review comment has a response and that every promised manuscript change exists. Rebuild and inspect the LaTeX output after source changes. When resubmitting to a different venue, use its supplied template and verify current official requirements rather than carrying over the previous venue's assumptions.
