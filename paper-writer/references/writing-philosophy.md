# English Argument and Prose Guide

Use this guide when drafting or revising an English CS/ML/AI paper section.

## Build an Argument Readers Can Test

Make the paper's central claim specific enough that a reader can identify the setting, technical change, and supporting evidence. A useful working statement names the problem, what changes, and the measured or derived consequence, but it is a planning aid rather than a mandatory manuscript sentence. Let the argument determine the section order: the reader needs the problem and distinction before evaluating the method, and the method before evaluating the evidence.

At paragraph scale, give each paragraph a job. It may establish context, formulate a gap, explain a mechanism, introduce an experiment, interpret a result, or set a limit. Connect the job to the prior paragraph so the transition expresses a real relation, such as contrast, consequence, refinement, or scope.

## Place Information Where It Helps

Keep the grammatical subject close to its action when possible. Begin a sentence with context the reader can recognize, then place the new or consequential information where it receives emphasis. Prefer an explicit actor and verb when a nominalized construction hides the action. These are revision prompts, not mechanical tests: preserve a sentence when its structure best serves the technical explanation.

Name referents precisely when a pronoun could point to more than one method, result, or object. Use one stable term for a technical entity after defining it. Define notation before depending on it, and introduce an equation with its role in the argument rather than presenting it without context.

## Match Claim Strength to Evidence

Use specific metrics, settings, and comparisons when they are available. Qualify a claim when the data, derivation, or evaluation only supports a narrower scope. A result can demonstrate an outcome under the reported protocol without proving an explanation for that outcome. State interpretations as interpretations and distinguish them from measured findings.

When describing prior work, credit its relevant strength before identifying the tradeoff that matters for the present setting. When describing the present method, explain the mechanism that connects the design to the intended capability. When describing a limitation, state the affected condition and its implication for the claim.

## Revise for Meaning

Remove repetition, generic emphasis, and transitions that do not state a relationship. Retain a term when it carries precise technical meaning; no vocabulary list substitutes for reading the paragraph in context. After editing, read the passage with adjacent paragraphs and ask whether a knowledgeable reader can identify the claim, evidence, referents, and remaining scope without inferring unstated facts.
